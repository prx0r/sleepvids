# ALGORITHM — RAW RESEARCH DUMP

## ETYMOLOGY

### Etymonline
algorithm (n.) 1690s, "Arabic system of computation," from French algorithme, refashioned (under mistaken connection with Greek arithmos "number") from Old French algorisme "the Arabic numeral system" (13c.), from Medieval Latin algorismus, a mangled transliteration of Arabic al-Khwarizmi "native of Khwarazm" (modern Khiva in Uzbekistan), surname of the mathematician whose works introduced sophisticated mathematics to the West. The earlier form in Middle English was algorism (early 13c.), from Old French. The meaning broadened to any method of computation; from mid-20c. especially with reference to computing.

### Wiktionary etymology tree
Arabic ال (al-) → Old Persian *u-v-a-r- → Old Persian *z-mi-i- → Old Persian u-v-a-r-z-mi-i-y → Old Persian u-v-a-r-z-mi-i-š → Classical Persian خْوَارَزْم (xwārazm) → Arabic خَوَارِزْم (ḵawārizm) → Arabic خَوَارِزْمِيّ (ḵawārizmiyy) → Arabic الخَوَارِزْمِيّ (al-ḵawārizmiyy) → Medieval Latin algorismus → Anglo-Norman algorisme → Middle English algorisme → English algorism → English algorithm

The spelling change (-s- > -th-) was influenced by unrelated Ancient Greek ἀριθμός (arithmós) "number."

### The spelling corruption
The word was originally "algorism" (from al-Khwarizmi's name). In the 1690s, it was "refashioned" to "algorithm" under a mistaken connection with Greek arithmos "number." The -th- is a lie — it has nothing to do with arithmetic. It's a mangled transliteration of a Persian mathematician's name.

### The algebra connection
From Etymonline: algebra (n.) "formal mathematics; the analysis of equations," 1550s, from Medieval Latin algebra, from Arabic "al-mukhtasar fi hisab al-jabr wa al-muqabala" ("the compendium on calculation by restoring and balancing"), the title of the famous 9c. treatise on equations by Baghdad mathematician Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi. Arabic al jabr "reunion of broken parts" (reducing fractions to integers in computation) was one of the two preparatory steps to solving algebraic equations.

The same man gave us both "algorithm" AND "algebra." Two words, one person, two books.

## AL-KHWARIZMI — THE MAN

### Born
c. 780 CE in Khwarazm (modern-day Khiva, Uzbekistan). Persian family. The region was near the Aral Sea in south-central Asia.

### The House of Wisdom
Al-Khwarizmi was invited by Caliph al-Ma'mun to join the House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma) in Baghdad, the most advanced research center of its time. He worked there between 813 and 833 CE. The House of Wisdom was a scientific research and teaching center where scholars translated Greek and Indian works.

From Wikipedia: Under the Caliph al-Ma'mun (reigned 813-833) al-Khwarizmi became a member of the "House of Wisdom" (Dar al-Hikma), a kind of academy of scientists set up at Baghdad.

### His books
1. Al-Kitab al-mukhtasar fi hisab al-jabr wa-l-muqabala (The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing) — c. 820 CE. This gave us "algebra" (from al-jabr).

2. Algoritmi de numero Indorum (The Book of Indian Numerals) — introduced Hindu-Arabic numerals (0-9) to the West. This gave us "algorithm" (from his name).

### His contributions
- Founded algebra as a separate mathematical discipline
- Introduced Hindu-Arabic numerals (including zero) to the West
- Created systematic methods for solving linear and quadratic equations
- Made contributions to trigonometry, astronomy, geography
- Used geometry to explain algebraic concepts

### The irony
From research: "Al-Khwarizmi's algorithms were used for almost three centuries, until replaced by Al-Uqlidisi's algorithms that could be carried out with pen and paper."

His name survived even after his methods were replaced. The word outlived the work.

## THE JOURNEY OF THE WORD

### 780 CE: Birth
Born in Khwarazm (now Khiva, Uzbekistan). His name means "the Chorasmian" — from Khwarazm, the region.

### 820 CE: The book
Writes his algebra treatise in Baghdad. The book is translated into Latin in the 12th century.

### 12th century: Latin translation
His name is Latinized as "Algoritmi" (or "Algorismi"). The Latin text becomes Algoritmi de numero Indorum. European mathematicians start using his methods.

### 13th century: Old French
Algorismus becomes "algorisme" in Old French. The word enters Middle English as "algorisme" (early 13th century). It means "the Arabic numeral system."

### 1690s: The corruption
The word is "refashioned" from "algorism" to "algorithm" under a mistaken connection with Greek arithmos "number." The -th- is added. The word's origin is obscured.

### 1930s: The broadening
The word broadens from "Arabic numeral computation" to "any method of computation."

### 1950s: Algol
The computer language Algol is coined from "algo(rithmic) l(anguage)."

### Mid-20th century: Computing
The word becomes associated with computing. "The algorithm" becomes shorthand for the software system that controls your feed.

### 2018+: The demon
Donald Glover thanks "the great algorithm." Uber drivers call it "the boss." The word that meant a man from Uzbekistan now means the invisible force that decides what you see, what you buy, who you meet.
