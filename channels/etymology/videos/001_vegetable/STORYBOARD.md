## Scene 4: The Root
**Time:** 7:00-15:00 | **Words:** ~840
**Tone:** Deep, follow-the-thread
**Content:** Full Etymonline entry for vegetable. Wiktionary etymology tree. PIE *weg- → Latin vegere → vegetus → vegetare → vegetabilis → Old French → Middle English. Read the entries line by line. Unpack each stage.

**Key passages:**
- Etymonline: "early 15c., 'capable of life or growth; growing, vigorous'"
- Wiktionary: "From Middle English vegetable, from Old French vegetable, from Latin vegetābilis"
- *weg- entry: "to be strong, be lively"

## Scene 6: The Word Enters English
**Time:** 25:00-32:00 | **Words:** ~735
**Tone:** Historical, descriptive
**Content:** Old English wyrt. How vegetable displaced it. The word entering in the 1400s meaning "any plant." The Old French route. The Norman Conquest connection. Trees were vegetables. Roses were vegetables.

**Key passage:**
- Etymonline: "The Old English word was wyrt... vegetable displaced it."

## Scene 7: The Narrowing — 1767
**Time:** 32:00-40:00 | **Words:** ~840
**Tone:** The turning point, slow realization
**Content:** The word narrows from "any plant" to "edible plant." Agriculture specializes. Linnaeus classifies. The Enlightenment love of taxonomy. 1767 — first recorded use as "food." What was lost.

**Key passage:**
- Etymonline: "the specific sense of 'plant cultivated for culinary purposes, edible herb or root' is recorded by 1767"

## Scene 8: The Decline — 1854 to 1976
**Time:** 40:00-50:00 | **Words:** ~1,050
**Tone:** The word losing its power
**Content:** 1854: "dull, uneventful." 1921: "person who leads a monotonous life." 1972: "persistent vegetative state." 1976: "one totally incapacitated mentally and physically." Track each shift. The word goes from alive → food → dull → brain-dead.

**Key passages:**
- Etymonline: "The sense of 'dull, uneventful' is attested from 1854"
- Adams & Fins: "the phrase 'vegetative' first appeared in Aristotle's treatise On the Soul"

## Scene 9: Aristotle — The Man
**Time:** 50:00-58:00 | **Words:** ~840
**Tone:** Philosophical, biographical
**Content:** Who was Aristotle. The Lyceum. De Anima. What he was trying to do. Read the MIT OCW passage. "By life we mean self-nutrition and growth and decay."

**Key passage:**
- MIT OCW: "Aristotle takes it for granted that to have a soul is to be alive: and 'by life we mean self-nutrition and growth and decay'. So even plants have a kind of psychē."

## Scene 10: Aristotle — The Threefold Soul
**Time:** 58:00-70:00 | **Words:** ~1,260
**Tone:** Reading Aristotle, unpacking line by line
**Content:** Read the actual passages from De Anima. Vegetative, sensitive, rational. The hierarchy. Each level contains the ones below it. The medieval reception. Aquinas. The chain of being.

**Key passages:**
- Aristotle: "the soul must a substance in the sense of the form of a natural body having life potentially within it" (412a19-21)
- MIT OCW: "The soul of an apple tree brings apple tree capacities; the soul of a dog, dog capacities; the soul of a human, human capacities."

## Scene 12: The Medical Hijacking
**Time:** 80:00-90:00 | **Words:** ~1,050
**Tone:** The twist, the loop closing
**Content:** Adams & Fins 2016. Bichat, Timme, the "vegetative nervous system." Jennett and Plum 1972. Read the full Adams & Fins abstract. The word's journey from Aristotle's soul to a hospital room. The NEJM definition.

**Key passages:**
- Adams & Fins: "Coined as a diagnostic category by Scottish neurosurgeon Bryan Jennett and American neurologist Fred Plum in 1972, the phrase 'vegetative' first appeared in Aristotle's treatise On the Soul"
- NEJM 1994: "The vegetative state is a clinical condition of complete unawareness of the self and the environment"

## Scene 13: The Misdiagnosis
**Time:** 90:00-95:00 | **Words:** ~525
**Tone:** Slow reveal, unsettling
**Content:** Bender et al. 2015. 37-43% misdiagnosis rate. 10-24% can regain consciousness. Read the key findings. The word hides more than it reveals.

**Key passage:**
- Bender: "Contrary to prior assumptions, 10% to 24% of patients in PVS can regain consciousness, sometimes years after the event"

## Scene 14: Plants Are Intelligent
**Time:** 95:00-108:00 | **Words:** ~1,365
**Tone:** The turnaround, wonder
**Content:** Gagliano 2014 — Mimosa pudica learns. Calvo 2020 — "Plants are intelligent." Mancuso — "Aristotle was wrong." Read the abstracts. Read the key quotes. The word remembers what Aristotle forgot.

**Key passages:**
- Gagliano: "Astonishingly, Mimosa can display the learned response even when left undisturbed in a more favourable environment for a month."
- Calvo: "Intelligence is a property that an individual plant has as it interacts with its environment."
- Mancuso: "The idea that plants are low-level organisms dates back to Aristotle... Today we know that plants are much more sophisticated in sensing than animals."
