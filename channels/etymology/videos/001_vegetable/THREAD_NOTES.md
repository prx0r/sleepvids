# THREAD NOTES — what to expand and why

## Section 2: The Root Family
Currently 1,268 words. Need ~1,890. ~600 more.

Threads to follow:
- **The Laws of Ælfred passage** — read the Old English, translate it, unpack it. This is a real document from 890. Charming and dark.
- **Witch → Wicca → modern paganism** — the word went from necromancer to religion. That's a fun thread.
- **The night watch** — medieval cities had actual night watchmen. The word watch comes from staying awake. Could paint a scene.

## Section 4: Aristotle
Currently 1,259 words. Need ~3,150. ~1,900 more.

Threads to follow:
- **Who was Aristotle** — not just what he wrote. His life. He was Alexander the Great's tutor. He walked in the Lyceum. He collected specimens. He was a biologist before he was a philosopher.
- **De Anima passages** — read actual passages from the text, line by line. The listener can handle it. It's interesting.
- **The analogy of the axe** — Aristotle argues the soul is to the body as sharpness is to the axe. An axe that can't cut doesn't have sharpness. A body that can't live doesn't have a soul. This is charming.
- **Medieval reception** — how monks and scholars read Aristotle. The chain of transmission. Arabic → Latin. Avenues of knowledge.
- **Aquinas** — the greatest medieval mind, building on Aristotle. The angelic doctor. His synthesis.

## Voice notes
- When reading a passage, slow down. Let each clause land.
- When a surprising fact arrives, pause before the next sentence.
- Use questions the listener is already thinking: "How did that happen?"
- Don't be afraid to say "Here's the thing" or "Think about that."
- The occasional dry observation: "Now it means broccoli."
- When describing a scene (a garden, a hospital, an ancient school), be specific. Not "a garden" but "every monastery had a garden."
