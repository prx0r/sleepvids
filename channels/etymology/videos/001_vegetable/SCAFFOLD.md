# SCAFFOLD — "The Word 'Vegetable'"

## The one-sentence story
A word that meant "alive" traveled through five thousand years of human thought and became the word for the worst thing that can happen to a human mind — and modern science proved the original meaning was right all along.

## The arc

```
PIE *weg- "to be strong"
    ↓
Latin vegetus "vigorous, enlivened"
    ↓
Old French vegetable "living, fit to live"
    ↓
Middle English "any plant" (1400s)
    ↓
"plant cultivated for food" (1767) ← THE NARROWING
    ↓
"dull, uneventful" (1854) ← THE DECLINE
    ↓
"persistent vegetative state" (1972) ← THE HIJACKING
    ↓
"one totally incapacitated" (1976) ← THE BOTTOM
    ↓
"Plants are intelligent" (2014-2020) ← THE TURNAROUND
    ↓
The word remembers what Aristotle forgot
```

## The loops

### Loop 1: Alive → Not Alive
vegetable (adj.) 1400s: "capable of life or growth; growing, vigorous"
vegetable (n.) 1976: "one totally incapacitated mentally and physically"
Same word. Opposite meanings. 576 years.

### Loop 2: Aristotle's Ghost
Aristotle coined "vegetative" for the lowest soul (350 BCE)
Jennett and Plum used it for the worst medical condition (1972)
2,300 years later, his classification named the unthinkable

### Loop 3: The Root Family Kept Its Power
*weg- gave us: vigour (still strong), wake (still awake), watch (still watching), vigil (still alert)
But vegetable lost its power. From "to be strong" to "broccoli"

### Loop 4: Science Proves Aristotle Wrong
Aristotle said plants have only the vegetative soul — the lowest level
Gagliano showed plants can learn. Mancuso showed they communicate.
The word carries a hierarchy that science has demolished

### Loop 5: The Old English Word It Killed
wyrt meant "root, herb, plant, spice"
Vegetable displaced it
Then vegetable lost its meaning
The word that killed wyrt became what wyrt described
